# 05 – Develop concise, directional copy

## Begin where the visitor is

The owner understands the solution much better than a new visitor. Do not compress that whole mental model into the opening text. Lead with a recognizable concern; let the following story unfold unfamiliar possibilities. In this project, loss of customer experience to chat was a headliner; repeated context and disconnected personal assistants belonged farther down the argument.

Useful hero formula:

1. **Eyebrow:** what this page is about.
2. **Fear/question:** understandable on its own.
3. **Action/resolution:** short, confident, contrasting color.
4. **Supporting text:** enough context to invite reading, not a summary of every solution capability.

For example, the current Agentic Web hero asks “Are your customers / moving to chat?” and answers “Reclaim the / user experience”. The explanation stays brief. The white/left and gold/right treatment, centered supporting text and explicit line breaks are this project's visual choices, not mandatory rules for every website.

## Collaborate in small, faithful rounds

- Review one page, from the top down, starting with the owner's direct statements. If they supply rough wording, improve it locally before proposing unrelated slogans.
- When asked to restate five candidates with three changes, make those three changes and leave the rest alone. Don't silently polish wording marked “verbatim”.
- Bank candidates in an existing brainstorming section. Mark the active choice and what is final versus still being tried. Do not resurrect rejected phrases while discussing the next section.
- Distinguish tentative “I think I like this” from final approval. Attribute assistant-written language accurately when asked where it came from.
- Show the headline, action and supporting text together. After narrowing the options, test the copy in real desktop and phone screenshots; text that feels short in chat can dominate a hero.
- A line-break adjustment is not authorization to change words. A layout experiment does not reopen approved copy.
- Lock accepted passages promptly in the current draft's `copy.md` and record the decision in the backlog. Say explicitly if HTML implementation is still pending.

## Direction before feature count

Turn competency lists into **3–5 directional headings**, not seven parallel features. Problem and solution need not map one-to-one.

For Agentic Web, the sequence became:
- Answers replace visits; chat takes over the experience; the platform and the customer's assistant hold different pieces of the experience.
- Make the platform discoverable/addressable; be useful where conversations start and lead inward; enable a richer shared platform experience.

This is not a template argument for workflows or factories. Research those pains and outcomes independently.

## Two levels of story text

**Left:** stable outline headings that tell the story when skimmed.

**Right:** a concise paragraph, useful quote, or numerical point with matching imagery. One heading can remain highlighted through several moments. These moments are not a numbered list or a visible bullet wall.

Introduce terminology before relying on it. The assistant's context, capability and awareness were developed across three passages rather than packed into a jargon-heavy title. Don't add tiny gold micro-headings merely to label each passage; the owner rejected that extra visual vocabulary.

Use separate moments only when they earn their time. Solution 2's integration and invitation were ultimately merged into one paragraph/slide. An extra moment that changes little but adds scrolling is not automatically better storytelling.

Draft format:

```text
### Short left-outline heading
Moment 1 – visible copy
[Brief paragraph, including source attribution where needed]
Graphic direction: [specific scene, not visible prose]
Source/editorial guardrail: [internal only]
```

## Voice, emphasis and sources

- Confident, direct, brief. Remove redundant setup. Avoid generic AI promises and anthropomorphic claims such as “your product knows”.
- Don't explain obvious conditions on the page when they drain confidence; keep technical limitations internally. Never gain confidence by falsifying evidence.
- Quoted buyer/expert language can carry a whole moment. Retain exact words and attribution; link the attribution itself, not a superscript number.
- Integrate statistics only where useful. The 8%/15% finding survived; “Barely half the click-through rate” was later removed as unnecessary repetition. Don't turn it into a claim that overall traffic halved.
- For this project, use **spaced en dashes (` – `), not em dashes**. Headline periods are optional; keep the finality the owner likes in “You're here for a reason.” and the question mark where requested.
- White/gold can express contrast inside a **single paragraph**. Exact clauses can be colored or italicized without adding labels or breaking the flow. Make sure nested bold text inherits the intended gold rather than a blanket white `strong` style overriding it.

## Transitions, proof and conversion

A transition can be a full scene with reading dwell, not an explanatory paragraph. Current transition: “Chat is where the conversation starts / Make your platform where it continues.” Don't insert a new metaphor or explain the vision again.

Treat proof as a whole composition: heading, books, testimonials and authorship. “Give it a slide” did not mean splitting off a title card or compacting the existing layout. Preserve quotes and supplied roles. Arcturus is the entity; John is the founder and **coauthor**, not sole author, of the books. Current captions are not publisher labels: one shared sentence names and links both book titles to Amazon.

Use only the testimonial text and identity supplied or reliably sourced. John eventually supplied Arin Sime's exact quote and CEO/Founder at AgilityFeat attribution; a video's unattributed narrator was not enough to assign that quote earlier. General publication permission is a separate check.

For story pages, omit distracting alternate-path sections and unrelated posts/videos. The homepage still has a chooser and broader content/proof role. Closing copy is simple: a large “Build what comes next”, a booking button, and **one** subscription invitation. Avoid two competing labels such as “Follow the work / New posts from…”. A visually hidden email label can preserve accessibility without redundant visible prose.

## Deliverable

The active draft's `copy.md` should contain the full current copy, sources and clearly separated editorial notes. Remove superseded visible text instead of leaving it below a stale “snapshot” label. Preserve alternatives in brainstorming, not as apparently current page copy. Update HTML only within the requested scope; keep the copy document and implementation synchronized once assembled.
