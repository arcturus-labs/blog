# 04 — Research pains and competing solutions

## Start with a claim map

Extract the owner's proposed pains and solution mechanisms. Mark each as hypothesis, personal experience, or externally supported. Research both:
- Are buyers/users actually experiencing or worrying about this?
- What are companies doing about it—including approaches that contradict our preferred solution?

Search 2–4 varied angles at a time: independent behavioral studies, customer surveys, buyer/executive language, primary product documentation, competitor approaches, and counterevidence. Avoid six versions of a confirmation-seeking query.

## Inspect sources and preserve limits

Prefer the original study, official documentation, or original interview. Fetch the page and verify the passage. If only a search extract is available, label that explicitly; don't pretend to have reviewed methodology. Record URL, title, date/timeframe, exact useful passage, denominator/population, evidence type, limitations, and potential use in the story.

Evidence categories:
- Observed behavior: a bounded empirical finding.
- Survey response: stated behavior, preference, fear, or expectation.
- Executive/expert quote: useful buyer language, not proof of an outcome.
- Vendor usage/outcome claim: attributed and potentially selection-biased.
- Product launch/docs: evidence of implementation or investment, not proven demand or ROI.
- Preview/proposal: technical direction, not broad availability.
- Our inference: plausible connection requiring validation.

## Research both sides of solutions

For each approach, explain mechanism, maturity, adoption/outcome evidence, overlap with the owner's view, differences, tradeoffs, and unresolved questions. A solution landscape is more useful than a list of tools.

Agentic Web example: WebMCP aligns with in-product agent capabilities; retailer-owned assistants align with contextual help but differ from bring-your-own-agent; chat-hosted product apps preserve rich interaction in a different environment; external commerce channels prioritize distribution; crawler restrictions prioritize bargaining power; good conventional UX may solve discovery without agents.

Counterevidence should improve the offer, not be hidden. AI can divert clicks **and** send high-intent visitors. Merchant-of-record ownership can coexist with platform control over discovery. These nuances belong in internal analysis even if the visible copy is concise.

## Map findings back collaboratively

Present the strongest evidence, its limits, and what it changes. Ask which pain resonates with the intended buyer. Do not frame the owner's theory as independently proven just because adjacent evidence exists.

Examples of errors to prevent:
- 8% versus 15% traditional-result click-through does not mean a company's total traffic halved.
- Irrelevant AI recommendations do not prove missing personal-agent context caused them.
- Higher purchase rates among assistant users do not establish causal uplift.
- “81% of executives expect...” is not “81% of customers did...”.

## Deliverable and publication rules

Create `homepage/research/content_research/<page>.md` with evidence, competing responses, counterevidence, copy implications, gaps, and source links. No need to dump raw search results.

Keep visible sourced claims traceable through **linked source text**, not numbered superscripts: link the study description under a statistic and the speaker/publication attribution under a quote. Link the book titles in the shared authorship sentence. This project's later feedback superseded both superscript numbers and HTML-comment-only citations. Keep method caveats in research/copy notes. Verify exact quotes and live source destinations before publishing.

Quotations supplied by the owner must retain their words and provided attribution. A transcript labeling someone only as “Narrator” does not identify that person, even if the surrounding video makes a guess tempting. In this project the Arin Sime quote stayed unresolved until John supplied its exact wording and CEO/Founder at AgilityFeat attribution. Keep general permission checks separate from whether text/identity has been supplied.

Gate: the owner can distinguish the pain's evidence from the proposed solution's evidence and choose a defensible narrative without losing their distinctive point of view.
