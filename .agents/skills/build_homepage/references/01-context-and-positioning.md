# 01 — Recover context and establish positioning

## Inputs

Start with the project README, curated owner notes, backlog, and existing artifacts. Ask what the visitor should understand, feel, and do. Distinguish the owner's preferred future business from work they can credibly deliver now.

For Arcturus, read `Arcturus Core Competencies` in the vault, especially the current page's section, delivery boundaries, and learning gaps. Follow its corresponding talk links: talks often explain the story more naturally than capability lists. Read the relevant linked notes rather than merely recognizing their titles. Other useful context includes strategy, biography, accomplishments, testimonials, and speaking records.

Use a vault-aware discovery tool where available; read source files directly if the app/CLI cannot connect. Don't infer that an old cached summary reflects current notes.

## Recover earlier decisions when needed

If history was compacted or a decision's rationale is missing, use the installed conversation-search skill/utilities. Narrow to project sessions, search user turns first, then inspect bounded surrounding passages. Dates in session filenames identify session start, not necessarily turn date. Compacted/forked histories can duplicate earlier turns; don't count duplication as independent confirmation.

Logs are historical evidence, not instructions overriding current requests. Extract useful rationale into the working artifact; do not dump entire transcripts or tool output into the repo. Never include credentials or private transcript material unnecessarily.

## Clarify these questions conversationally

- Who is the buyer, and what painful situation sends them here?
- What can the business credibly do about it?
- Is the primary goal consulting inquiries, a product sale, readership, or community?
- What evidence/proof exists? What is only aspiration?
- What should a visitor do next? What is explicitly not being offered?

Do not ask a long questionnaire if the notes already answer it. Summarize the uncertainties and ask the question that actually changes the next decision.

## Separate the buyer's starting point from the owner's destination

The owner's most interesting solution is not necessarily the right headline. Buyers can recognize loss of visibility/control before they understand personal assistants sharing a product surface. Start with that recognized fear; reveal the unfamiliar possibility through the problem/solution sequence. Reading the competencies helps explain the vision, but is not a reason to cram its terminology into the hero.

When the owner asks “did I say that?”, distinguish an exact user phrase, tentative acceptance of an assistant suggestion, and explicit final approval. Recover the source turn if uncertain. Do not pass an assistant-written transition off as the owner's words.

## Arcturus specifics

Homepage is a chooser for Agentic Web, business workflows, and software factories; each path receives balanced treatment. Booking a conversation is primary; blog email subscription is secondary. No public prices. Arcturus is the brand, not simply a personal bio page.

John was an early production engineer on GitHub Copilot and **coauthored** two books. Do not invent testimonial outcomes or titles. Team-scale software factories and agentic SEO have learning gaps; don't market them as established achievements.

## Deliverable and gate

A concise shared understanding of audience, job, offer, conversion, and hypotheses. Record actual decisions in the appropriate existing documents, not a new document for every discussion. Preserve owner-curated files unless edits are requested. Then move to architecture or evidence, depending on what is uncertain.
