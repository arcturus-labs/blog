## Problems

### 1. Your experts are wasting time on routine tasks.

*“Routine tasks” is the current plain-language draft alternative to “ancillary tasks,” not a final headline approval. Reuse the expert amid paperwork with opacity raised slightly from 27% to 33%. Three evidence moments share the same stationary image.*

**41%** of the working day

Desk workers in Slack’s survey said that 41% of their working day went to tasks that were “low value, repetitive or lack meaningful contribution to their core job functions.”

[Slack Workforce Lab · 10,281 desk workers · January 2024](https://slack.com/blog/news/new-slack-research-shows-accelerating-ai-use-at-work)

**4.9 hours** per week.

Knowledge workers estimated better processes could save them 4.9 hours per week. Asana’s survey found {gold:58% of time went to “work about work.”}

[Asana Anatomy of Work · 9,615 knowledge workers · 2023](https://investors.asana.com/news-releases/news-release-details/asana-anatomy-work-global-index-2023-smart-collaboration-and)

**60–70%** of employee time

McKinsey estimated that generative AI and other technologies could automate activities occupying 60–70% of employees’ time.

[McKinsey Global Institute · technical automation potential · 2023](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier)

## The solution

### 1. Start with the most impactful tasks.

Look for valuable, repeatable tasks with outcomes your experts can check. Watch how employees already use AI, then {gold:capture the most promising processes as agent skills.}

*Image: low-hanging fruit.*

### 2. Augment your expert staff.

Let agents perform research, make the easy decisions, and prepare drafts. {gold:Give experts the sources, findings, and open questions they need to review the work instead of doing it all from scratch.}

*Image stays the same for both solution-2 moments: a short adult-proportioned office-assistant robot with a sensor-visor head reaches upward to pass drafts to a taller human, who looks down toward its visor.*

*Next moment, still under solution 2:*

> “The output provides a genuinely strong starting point, allowing our lawyers to spend more time on the strategic judgement and client counsel that matter most.”

Joe Choy, Partner, Johnson Stokes & Master · [Microsoft News](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/features/from-legacy-to-ai-frontier-how-johnson-stokes-master-built-a-governance-first-copilot-model-for-regulated-legal-services/)

*Removed from the page this round (kept in research notes for possible later use): the 15% Generative AI at Work finding (5,172 customer-support workers) and the JSM 25–35% rework / one-to-two-hours figures. Research notes record that 25–35% measures rework during supervisory review, not total time preparing advice.*

### 3. Promote what proves reliable.

Capture expert corrections, classify and catalogue tasks, and {gold:fully automate tasks that are determined to be low-risk and highly dependable.}

*Image: confident human reviewing on the left; office-assistant robot working at a desk on the right.*

### 4. Maintain and continuously improve workflows.

Extract evaluation benchmarks from your experts’ daily work. When models, tools, or approaches change, {gold:rerun the benchmarks to ensure workflow quality stays on track.}

*“Ensure” is used instead of the draft’s “insure.” Image: the lowest office-assistant robot hands paperwork up to the next robot on an ascending staircase.*
