# Rachel Woods / The AI Exchange: review notes

Reviewed 2026-10-29, desktop 1280px only. Pages: home, bootcamp page. Files: `text.md` (raw page text; optional), `screenshots/01-home-fullpage.png`.
Business type: **training/certification company**, not a consulting site. Useful for packaging and conversion, less for consulting positioning.

## Above the fold
- Large headline: "The future of work runs on AI playbooks." Pale lavender band, centered, one sentence of explanation, one lime-green button: "Get Your Free Playbook Template."
- States what it teaches ("operationalize" AI) but not exactly who it's for in the first screen. Audience ("Operators, Consultants & Team Leads") appears lower.
- Press logos (Forbes, Fortune, etc.) sit immediately below the first screen.

## Navigation and structure
- Almost no navigation: logo + one button ("Become an AI Operator"). No about page, blog, or menu on the home page.
- Single scrolling page with 6 bands: hero, logos, free-template signup, certified-operator offer, reviews, newsletter.

## How the offer is packaged
- **One paid product** with a name, audience, three outcomes, a price ($3,000), and a button. A free lead magnet (playbook template) feeds it.
- A named method (CRAFT framework, "playbooks", "AI Operator") gives the brand a vocabulary.
- Two-step ladder: free template -> newsletter -> $3,000 certification.

## Proof
- Press/podcast logo strip, three star-rated quotes on the home page, and ~20 more on the bootcamp page.
- Testimonials use first name + last initial only. No titles, companies, or photos, so they feel less credible than quotes with roles. Each has a headline that works as a skim summary.
- A large photo of Rachel speaking sits behind the reviews.

## Conversion
- **Strongest of the sites reviewed so far.** Three email captures on one page (free template form, newsletter form, nav button), all plain: first name + email.
- Single primary action in the hero; repeated near the middle; newsletter again at the bottom.
- Only the free template and newsletter capture emails. There's no contact form or sales call on the home page, which fits a self-serve product.

## Voice and tone
- Friendly, confident, benefits-first. Short punchy statements ("Your next edge isn't AI. It's writing the playbook for it."). Playful mascot illustration. Corporate-ish vocabulary ("operationalize", "certified").

## Agent-readiness
- Cloudflare; robots.txt present (Cloudflare content-signals preamble). 404 for sitemap, llms.txt, `.well-known` files, RSS. No JSON-LD found.
- Not agent-ready beyond basics.

## Freshness
- Footer says (c) 2025; the bootcamp page is a live product page. No blog or dated content to judge by.

## Takeaways relevant to Arcturus
- Clear, repeatable conversion structure (free lead magnet -> newsletter -> offer) is worth studying.
- A named framework and a single priced offer make "what do you sell" obvious, at the cost of narrowing to one product.
- Reviews without roles are weak; use roles (CEO/CTO) as you noted.
- Visual style: soft pastels and a mascot, a deliberate contrast with a "serious consultant" look.
