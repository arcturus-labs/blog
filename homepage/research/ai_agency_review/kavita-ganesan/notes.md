## Above the fold

## Navigation and structure
- 7 nav items, including a link to a separate company site (Advisory & Consulting) and a separate book site. Content is spread over three domains: personal, company, and book.
- Page flow: hero -> credentials -> About (long) -> newsletter -> four ways to work together -> book -> excerpts -> contact.

## How the offer is packaged
- **"Four ways to work together"**: AI Transformation Programs, Keynote Speaking, Executive/Team Coaching, Executive & Leadership Training. Each is two sentences and has a button.
- Not priced. "Transformation programs" is the core consulting offer, and it is described by outcomes (identify opportunities, readiness gaps, architect solutions).
- The book is the lead product and the main credential.

## Proof

- One testimonial, from a CIO, about the book rather than consulting. No client case studies on this page.

## Conversion
- Hero: newsletter and "Get expert advice." Mid-page newsletter form (name + email). Booking buttons for speaking/coaching. A separate contact page, not a form on the home page.
- Newsletter targets "executives and senior leaders in charge of managing innovation," so the audience is specified.
- Book sales are a second conversion path.

## Voice and tone

## Agent-readiness

## Freshness
- Book is promoted as a 2nd edition; the site seems maintained. I did not check blog dates.

## Takeaways relevant to Arcturus
- "Four ways to work together" is a clear, simple engagement menu and close to your delivery formats.
- The About text is a long wall of credentials; it works for boards but is hard to skim.
- The three-domain split makes the brand feel scattered, which is the risk you named.
- Naming a specific audience (executives) in the newsletter pitch is a good move.
