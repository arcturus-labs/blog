# Dave Ebbelaar / Datalumina: review notes

Reviewed 2026-10-29, desktop 1280px. Pages: daveebbelaar.com (personal), datalumina.com (company). Academy not reviewed. Files: `text.md` (raw page text; optional), `screenshots/01-personal-site-above-fold.png`, `screenshots/02-datalumina-above-fold.png`.
Two very different sites. Datalumina is a productized dev shop; the personal site is a deliberately minimal hub.

## Above the fold
**Datalumina:** dark navy, glowing, "AI-forward" look. Badge "Your AI development partner"; headline "Blazing fast builds for *AI-forward companies*"; one paragraph (decade of experience, "without the meetings, bloat, or delays of traditional agencies"); social-proof row ("Trusted by 50+ companies"); white button "Submit Project Request"; a testimonial card with face, stars, name, title and company. Nav has only Academy and "Start Your Project."
- Answers "what, for whom, how fast, who says so" in one screen. Very clear.
**daveebbelaar.com:** warm off-white, a small avatar and one paragraph ("I build software products, make videos, and teach..."). Quiet and personal, no pitch.

## Navigation and structure
- Datalumina: two nav items. One long scroll: hero, approach ("sprint"), pain/contrast, founder story, results, testimonials, CTA, footer.
- Personal: two nav items (Writing, Projects), one screen. Writing section reads "The first post is on its way."
- The two sites separate roles: personal brand/links vs. sales page vs. academy (courses on a third domain).

## How the offer is packaged
- **Strongly productized**: a "2-week MVP sprint," "live in 2 weeks," "limited to 2 new clients per month." Trust badges: Private Data, GDPR, 99.9% Uptime SLA.
- Positions against agencies and "strategy" work explicitly ("Most agencies sell ideas. We deliver products."). The contrast box ("You don't have to / Instead you could") is a clear pain-versus-promise pattern.
- No prices shown. Entry = submit a project request, then engineers review and "confirm if it's a fit."

## Proof
- Testimonial cards in the hero, mid-page banner, and a 7-item carousel, each with **name, role, and company** (Founder, Founder, CTO).
- **Two numeric results** with names: 38% support cost savings, 30% of tickets automated (BLANK); 2M+ visitors served, live prices under 200 ms (Crypto Insiders). One more: 43% cost reduction.
- Audience stats: 260k+ YouTube subscribers, 1M+ community, 10+ years. Clients are small startups, largely European.

## Conversion
- **Very strong.** One consistent action: "Start Your Project" / "Submit Project Request," repeated in the nav, hero, and closing section. Typeform-based request instead of an open contact form.
- Scarcity cue: "Limited to 2 new clients per month." Expectation-setting: a list of what you'll get from the first step.
- Email also visible in the footer (info@datalumina.com).

## Voice and tone
- Direct, confident, outcome-focused, slightly combative toward agencies. Short sentences. Founder section is personal and frames a problem ("95% of GenAI projects fail").
- Personal site: understated, plain, a little wry ("parts that still require some human brain activity").

## Agent-readiness
- **daveebbelaar.com has a real llms.txt** (markdown, summary, projects, links), RSS, sitemap. It is linked in the footer ("llms.txt").
- datalumina.com: sitemap yes; no llms.txt; mailto present; Vercel.
- No `.well-known` agent files on either.

## Freshness
- Datalumina footer: (c) 2026. Personal site has no posts yet (placeholder).

## Takeaways relevant to Arcturus
- Best conversion/packaging model so far: one outcome, a time-boxed offer, a pain/promise contrast, roles on testimonials, one repeated CTA.
- The dark "glowing blue" look is the generic AI-site style you want to avoid.
- Having the personal hub separate from the sales site is an option for the Arcturus/John split.
- A published llms.txt is a small step toward the agent-readiness story.
- Caution: the speed/MVP promise fits a build shop; your work (assessment, direction) frames differently.
